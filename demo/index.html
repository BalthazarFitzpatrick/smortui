toggle

One class for every clickable thing. A button, a list row, a filter pill and a dropdown head are all .toggle - which is what stops a page growing four kinds of button that are almost the same height. A dropdown head is a toggle with a caret and a menu attached; it is not a separate widget, and clicking this one opens a real menu that renames it.

what a button DOES, in colour

Only two verbs get a colour, because only two are worth stopping for: one adds, one takes away. Everything else stays grey on purpose - a palette where every button is coloured is a palette that has stopped saying anything. The colour sits on the border and the text at rest and only fills on hover, so a row still reads as a row of buttons rather than a row of warnings. Disabled wins over both, whatever verb it carries.

A grouped list with a status pip and trailing badges. Rows are .toggle like everything else; the badges push to the far edge so several rows' badges line up as a column rather than trailing their labels at ragged positions.

scrollbars

Drawn, not borrowed. macOS's overlay bar fades in over the content in a grey belonging to no palette; this is a track that is always there and a block that rides it, square like everything else. Measured: Chromium 151 no longer renders ::-webkit-scrollbar at all, so scrollbar-width and scrollbar-color lead and the webkit block is a fallback for older engines. Two things the standard property cannot express, and this therefore does not have: a 2px track line, and a thumb that lightens on hover.

scroll me — the bar to the right is the one base.css draws.

It is the same bar every panel, menu body and long list in the system gets, so nothing has to opt in and nothing looks like a different application.

slider

An axis with ticks rather than a number field, because a threshold is a position in a range. It takes an optional distribution drawn over the axis - the point being that "no results" and "no results because your cut sits above every value" look identical until something shows the shape.

rows that carry numbers

A menu row is a .toggle with a name and a .stats badge. The NAME truncates, not the stats - it used to be the other way round, and a long name pushed the row past the panel and squeezed the numbers to nothing. The stats are why you are reading the row, so they hold their width and pin right, which also lines them up down the column instead of trailing each name at a ragged position.

modal: the one popup NOT anchored to what opened it

Every other menu hangs off its trigger, which is right for a choice you can walk away from. This one is centred on a dimmed backdrop because it is a decision you must answer before anything else happens - overwrite, merge, or abandon. The three buttons carry the verbs above: green adds, red takes away, grey is the harmless exit, and Escape always picks the harmless one.

selection: click, cmd+click, shift+drag, right-click

Click picks one, cmd/ctrl+click adds, shift+drag draws a net, right-click acts on what you pointed at. A net, not a range. Selecting first-to-last through a rendered order only does what you want when the list happens to be sorted the way you are reading it; the mismatches you can actually see sit together on SCREEN, so a rectangle you drag is the honest gesture. The band is fixed-positioned and hit-tests in viewport coordinates, so no scroll offset or container origin has to be got right.

bucket layout, 2D roving focus

Arrow keys move across both axes: left/right between buckets, up/down between rows. Only the focused row sits in the tab order, the same roving-tabindex shell.js already uses for tabs. Moving into a shorter bucket clamps to its last row instead of losing focus; up from the top row of a bucket exits the grid rather than doing nothing.

card, and the fan

A card is a shape, not a subject: a title band reserved at two rows whether the title needs them or not, a rule, a body that takes the slack, a rule, and secondary facts on the floor. The two-row reserve is what keeps a wall of them reading as a grid rather than as a ragged pile.

The fan stacks them. Tab or click into one and it stays exactly where it is while the ones after it slide down — the item you are reading is never the item that moved. The overlap is a proportion rather than a pixel count, so the visible strip survives a narrow window. Collapsed, an 8px band of the page's own ground plus a cast that wraps the top corners is what stops the stack reading as one ruled list.

expand: a strip that opens into a panel

A landscape strip grows into a centred portrait panel, reusing .modal-backdrop and .panel-floating so it is not a second visual language for "floating over everything". Dismiss with a click on the backdrop or Escape.
